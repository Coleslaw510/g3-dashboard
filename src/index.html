How the numbers work
  • TDP: execution = commitments + obligations + disbursements. AFP and Allotment: execution = obligations + disbursements. Balance = funding level − execution.
  • Obligations and disbursements come from the Status of Funds export (same rules as the Aviation dashboard: signs flipped, obligation = total expenditure − disbursement, ALLOBJ rows dropped). Commitments come from the commitments report (Entry Type “commitment”, sign flipped). They are upcoming spending with no date yet, so charts show them in a separate “Upcoming” column, not in a month; they still count in TDP execution and balance.
  • Commitments report rows with Entry Type “obligation” are used only for spending by DPC (Status of Funds DPC table and the TDC/DPC breakouts). They never add to execution or balance, since the export already counts obligations.
  • Pace (TDP only) compares % executed with % of the fiscal year elapsed; within ±5 points is on pace.
  • Spend Plans and program tabs count actual spend as obligations + disbursements by fiscal month. The current month’s plan is prorated to the as-of date. Multiple prioritizations on one account are added together.
  • Unliquidated (FMZ3): open = Amount Open, days open = as-of date − Posting Date; zero-open lines are left out. Over 90 days is amber, over 180 red.
  • Pay group = the first 5 characters of the Functional Area when all 5 are digits (100101B3PRAT → 10010). Functional Areas like 131G48ASDF are another appropriation: no pay group, shown only under All. Used on the export, FMZ3 and the commitments report (which uses its Pay Group column first). Funding (TDP/AFP/Allotment) is added up per pay group by account: each account takes the pay group of its Functional Areas on the Accounts tab. An account with Functional Areas in more than one pay group is counted under the one it has most of and listed as a key conflict in Data details. Commitment items show their description from the Commitment Item tab, with the code in small grey text. The spend plan has no pay group.
